Sources & method

All figures are real IPEDS data, built directly from the NCES IPEDS Complete Data Files, using revised releases where NCES has published them. The build was reconciled field by field against the Urban Institute Education Data Portal, which republishes the same NCES IPEDS survey components. Components used: directory and Carnegie classification, 12-month enrollment (undergraduate FTE), student-to-faculty ratio, admissions, graduation rates (150% of normal time), fall retention, academic-year tuition, and student financial aid. See IPEDS survey components for definitions and the Carnegie Classification for the institution taxonomy.

  • Vintage. Enrollment, selectivity, retention and tuition are —. Graduation rates are — and financial-aid figures are —: NCES finalizes those components a year later, and only final releases are used.
  • Missing data. IPEDS suppresses or omits metrics that do not apply to an institution — open-admission colleges report no admit rate, and graduation rates require a qualifying cohort. Blank cells mean not reported, never zero. Medians are computed over reporting institutions only.
  • Retention and price. Retention is the full-time rate: first-time, full-time students who return the next fall. Part-time students are not included. Tuition and fees are published prices for full-time undergraduates before aid. In-district prices, which apply to local residents at many community colleges, are shown where they are lower than in-state prices.
  • Graduation rate. Completion within 150% of normal time: six years for four-year institutions, three years for two-year institutions. The two are not directly comparable, so use the Level filter before comparing them. Carnegie families such as Special Focus include both levels. The graduation KPI compares against the national median for the same level, and institution peer groups are drawn from the same level.
  • Peer groups. Peers are the nearest institutions in standardized space over log FTE, student-to-faculty ratio, Pell share and in-state tuition, restricted to the same control and Carnegie family — the peer-group method used in the companion repository.
  • Forecast. The dashed trend line is an ordinary least-squares fit on aggregate FTE for the current filter, extended three years. It is a descriptive trend, not a causal or institution-level projection.
